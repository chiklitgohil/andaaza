Question 1 of 6 · Phone model

Would Uber charge more if you hold an iPhone than a budget Android phone?

REPORTED ANECDOTE January 2025, India: viral posts showed one trip at ₹290.79 on Android and ₹342.47 on iPhone, about 18% more. India’s consumer regulator sent notices to Ola and Uber, and both denied it. These are screenshots, not a controlled study; fares also shift with driver supply within seconds. WION · Inc42

Question 2 of 6 · Battery

What about a phone that is almost out of battery?

Why this question: a person at 4% may be stuck and in a hurry, and may not shop around.

STATEMENT In 2016 Uber’s then research head said the company saw that low-battery users were more willing to accept surge pricing, and said Uber did not exploit it. REPORTED TEST A Brussels newspaper test found €17.56 at 12% battery against €16.60 at 84%. A US fact-check with under 20% against over 90% battery got identical prices. Uber denies using battery in fares. Two small tests that disagree prove nothing either way. Vice · Brussels Times · VERIFY

Question 3 of 6 · How you pay

Could the way you pay change the price: an Amex card, a gift-card balance, a plain debit card?

REPORTED ANECDOTE April 2026: a viral video showed an Atlanta UberX ride at $33.05 with an Amex selected and $20.33 after switching to a Visa (about +63%). Other riders say gift-card or Uber Cash balances made fares higher; one reader said a $20 ride rose to about $30. Uber told Fortune it never sets prices by card brand, gift card, Uber Cash, account balance or battery level. Forum posts are self-selected and can’t control for demand changes. Fortune · View from the Wing

Question 4 of 6 · Where you’re going

Could the neighbourhood you’re picked up from or dropped at change the price per mile?

STUDY (CORRELATION) Two George Washington University researchers analysed over 100 million Chicago ride-hailing trips (Nov 2018 to Dec 2019) and found higher fares per mile for pickups or drop-offs in neighbourhoods with larger non-white populations and higher poverty, and some other demographic links. They said it cannot show cause. Lyft called the analysis deeply flawed; Uber said it does not condone discrimination and pointed to factors such as time of day and land use. The size of the bump in the demo (+4%) is my invention; the study gives effect sizes in statistical units, not percentages. Block Club Chicago · GW · SSRC abstract

Question 5 of 6 · The “special price”

The app shows a crossed-out price and a lower “special” one. Is the discount always real?

REPORTED TEST 2026: Consumer Reports said about half of Uber and Lyft upfront prices it checked showed a discount, and that 11% of those were not real because of an inflated original price. A CR editor said Uber uses location and ride history to segment riders. Uber disputes CR’s method: a route median, it says, is the wrong reference because conditions change in seconds. Both sides agree prices vary by moment; they disagree on what the comparison means. Spectrum News

Question 6 of 6 · The Wi‑Fi you’re on

Could the Wi‑Fi network, or a weak signal, change what you’re quoted?

WHAT-IF I found no public report, test or study showing Wi‑Fi or signal strength sets ride prices. This question is here because some theories sound plausible (hotel Wi‑Fi means a traveller; weak signal means urgency) and it helps to notice how convincing a story can feel with no evidence behind it. “No evidence found” is not proof it never happens.
